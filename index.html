About

Welcome to my webpage!

I am an Assistant Professor of Economics at Toronto Metropolitan University, an Assistant Professor of Economics (Status-Only) at the University of Toronto, and a member of the Centre for Economic Evidence on Migration (CEEM).

I am a labour economist focusing on the economics of immigration. A central theme of my research examines how limited competition among employers—and the resulting wage-setting power—generates pay penalties for immigrants. For example, I investigate how institutional policies such as mobility restrictions, which prevent temporary foreign workers from freely changing employers, lead to lower wages, as well as how broader market frictions that limit outside options contribute to the immigrant pay gap. More broadly, my research analyzes the general equilibrium effects of immigration, exploring how labour markets, firms, and wages adjust in response to immigration inflows.

I received my PhD in Economics from the University of Toronto in 2025. I also hold an MA in Economics from the University of Michigan, a BSc in Financial Economics from the University of Toronto, and a BA in Criminology from Toronto Metropolitan University.
